    98 post a seizure-specific plan a family can find.

    Every point of light is one of Minnesota's 329 regular school districts, placed where its office is. The bright ones post a seizure-specific plan or page that a parent or a substitute teacher could find. That is about 30 percent.

    231

    The other 231 break apart.

    195 post only a general medication policy that never mentions seizures. 18 post nothing relevant, and 18 sites could not be checked. A plan may still be on file inside a district. We measured what a family can find.

    11% to 67%

    Sort them by size and the gap lines up.

    Under 500 students, 11 percent of districts post a plan. From 2,500 students up, 67 percent do. In public districts under 500 students, 79 percent have no licensed school nurse, which is why we send them a ready-made packet.

    For school districts: the free Seizure‑Safe Schools packet

    Everything a district needs to meet Minnesota's seizure-safety law (Minn. Stat. 121A.24) in one 7-page PDF: a seizure action plan template, drop-in Policy 516 language, a printable first-aid poster, a seizure observation log, and an adoption checklist. Free to copy, adapt, and use.

    What we do

    New here? Read about EDAN, what we do and why.

    About 53,700 Minnesotans had active epilepsy in 2015, by CDC estimates published in 2017. The state has not yet published a direct count. What goes wrong for them is usually visible in data that exists but nobody has assembled: a district with no plan, a pharmacy that closed, a specialist three hours away, a drug whose price rose 15 percent in a year. We pull that information out of state filings, federal registries, one-PDF-per-district reports and web archives, and we publish it with the method attached, so you can check it. Then we take it to the people who can act on it. Everything here is free to use.

    We started in Minnesota because we live here and because the questions were answerable. The method travels: the school audit rubric, the cost analysis and the distance mapping are all built from federal sources or state filings that every state has. Other states are next.

    Who we work for

    We collect the data and publish it with the method attached. The organizations that serve people with epilepsy, and the lawmakers who set the rules, decide what to do with it.

    • The Epilepsy Foundation of Minnesota, the Danny Did Foundation and other groups serving families: where the gaps are, by county and school district, for their programs and grants.
    • Legislators and their staff: sourced numbers for bills such as the epilepsy drug cost cap (HF 3652 / SF 3786) and a Minnesota SUDEP reporting law.
    • The Minnesota Department of Health's Epilepsy Program: district-level data the state does not collect itself.
    • School districts: the audit result for their own district and the free packet above.
