Research andtwo organizations

The projects page covers work I do alone. This page covers where I show up for other people, and where other people are counting on me.

Lab and clinical exposure

Lab assistant, Moriarty/Webber Lab

University of Minnesota Twin Cities. Ended September 2026.

I worked under Dr. Richa Kaushal in a gene editing and cell therapy lab, sitting under the Crane Lab at UMN. My day to day covered iPSC culture, differentiating iPSCs into macrophages, media prep, gene editing, passaging, and running maxipreps. I built my own study guide on iPSCs, iPSC derived macrophages, flow cytometry, and cell surface markers before starting, and I kept a running log of my shifts.

Neurosurgery observation and mentorship

Dr. Anant Naik, University of Minnesota

I have watched two live neurosurgeries under Dr. Naik, seeing each full procedure from inside the operating room. Through this mentorship I started looking into caregiver burden and communication gaps in glioblastoma (GBM) treatment, which turned into the NeuroBridge GBM project below.

NeuroBridge GBM

HOSA Medical Innovation Project

I am building a plan for caregiver communication and symptom tracking in glioblastoma care, for HOSA Medical Innovation. So far I have cold emailed more than 150 neuroscience and medical professionals for interviews and clinical feedback, and I have been running surveys and calls to collect real data on what caregivers actually struggle with.

BioSphere Learning and NeuroGIDAS

BioSphere Learning

Founder

An education hub I founded that connects students with tutoring organizations, so students looking for a tutor and tutoring programs looking for students both have one place to go. It has grown to more than 1,000 followers on Instagram.

NeuroGIDAS

Eastview High School, co-founder

I helped launch NeuroGIDAS, the neuroscience club at my school, and worked on its structure from the start so it would be more than a group that just meets to talk about the brain. I wrote the proposal that got it a faculty advisor, and I developed the concept behind a statewide Brain Cancer Innovation pitch competition, which the club is now working to actually run. The goal is to give students who are curious about neuroscience a real project to build.

Every project here starts from a public dataset and gets checked for the kind of leak that inflates most machine learning results on brain data. None of it is a medical device or medical advice.
