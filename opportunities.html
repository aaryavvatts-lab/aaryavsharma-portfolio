What I'mlooking for

I am a sophomore, so I know most of what follows is a couple of years early. I'd rather say what I want than wait until it's normal to ask.

The Twin Cities, where in person work is easiest for me. Photograph by Michael Barera, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Research positions

A neuroscience, neural engineering, or BCI lab where I can help with data analysis, literature review, or pipeline work. In person in the Twin Cities is easiest, but I can do computational work remotely from anywhere, and I already have a working setup for EEG, ECoG, fMRI, and MRI data.

Neurotech internships

An internship or informal summer role at a company working on brain-computer interfaces, EMG or EEG wearables, or neural monitoring devices. I am building my own concept in this space and want to learn from a team that has already shipped something.

Mentorship

A graduate student, postdoc, or founder willing to look at my work and tell me what I am getting wrong. I would rather hear it now than after I have built more on top of it.

Collaboration and competitions

Co-authoring a paper, entering a science fair or research competition together, or teaming up on a hackathon. I have the tooling and the habit of checking my own work, and I am looking for people who want to point that at a harder problem.

Email is the most reliable way to reach me. My contact details, resume, and GitHub are all on the contact page.
