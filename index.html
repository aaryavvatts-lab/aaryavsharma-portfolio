Most of what I do outside of class is take a public brain recording dataset and rebuild the analysis myself, to see if it holds up.

Ten projects are on this site. Eight start from brain or muscle recordings: EEG, ECoG, fMRI, MRI and EMG. Two are medical prediction demos I rebuilt from tutorials, to see how far the advertised accuracy holds. For each one I write up what worked, what did not, and why, and I put the code and a working demo online.

Until September 2026 I was a lab assistant in the Moriarty/Webber Lab at the University of Minnesota under Dr. Richa Kaushal, helping with iPSC culture, macrophage differentiation, and gene editing work. I have also watched two live neurosurgeries under Dr. Anant Naik.

Outside the lab, I am building a smartphone based peripheral nerve health screening concept and working toward starting a neurotech company. I founded BioSphere Learning, an education hub that connects students to tutoring organizations, and helped launch NeuroGIDAS, the neuroscience club at my school.

The number before the check, and after

Split the data the easy way and a model looks great. Split it the way a real device would see the world and the number usually drops, sometimes by half. Twice it went the other way: the textbook SSVEP method lost to a fix that read the whole spectrum, and a transformer lost to plain logistic regression. I check for this in every project, and it is usually the most important finding in the write-up.
