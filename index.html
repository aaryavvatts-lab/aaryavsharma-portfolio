One MRI slice from the OASIS dataset, from a person scored at the moderate stage.

Most of what I do outside of class is take a public brain recording dataset and rebuild the analysis myself, to see if it actually holds up. Ten projects are on this site. Eight start from brain or muscle recordings, EEG, ECoG, fMRI, MRI and EMG, and two are medical prediction demos rebuilt from tutorials to see how far the advertised accuracy holds. I write up what worked, what did not, and why, and I put the code and a working demo online for each one.

Until September 2026 I worked as a lab assistant in the Moriarty/Webber Lab at the University of Minnesota under Dr. Richa Kaushal, helping with iPSC culture, macrophage differentiation, and gene editing work. I have also watched two live neurosurgeries under Dr. Anant Naik.

Outside of the lab I am building a smartphone based peripheral nerve health screening concept, and I am working toward starting a neurotech company. I founded BioSphere Learning, an education hub connecting students to tutoring organizations, and helped launch NeuroGIDAS, the neuroscience club at my school.

The number before the check, and after

Split the data the easy way and a model looks great. Split it the way a real device would see the world and the number usually drops, sometimes by half. Twice it went the other way: the textbook SSVEP method lost to a fix that read the whole spectrum, and a transformer lost to plain logistic regression. I check for this in every project, and it is usually the most important finding in the write-up.
