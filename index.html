About

I check if impressive results really hold up.

Most of what I do outside class is take a public brain dataset and redo the analysis myself. I have done it ten times with EEG, ECoG, fMRI, MRI and EMG data, plus two medical prediction demos I rebuilt from tutorials to see how far the advertised accuracy holds. For each one I write up what worked and what did not, share the code, and put a working demo online.

Until September 2026, I was a lab assistant in the Moriarty/Weber Lab at the University of Minnesota under Dr. Richa Kaushal, helping with iPSC culture, macrophage differentiation and gene editing. I have also watched two live neurosurgeries with Dr. Anant Naik.

I am building a smartphone-based screening idea for peripheral nerve health, and I am working toward starting a neurotech company.

What I check

Numbers look great until you check them.

Split the data the easy way and a model looks great. Split it the way a real device would see the world and the number usually drops, sometimes by half. In two projects it went the other way: the textbook SSVEP method lost to a fix that read the whole spectrum, and a transformer lost to plain logistic regression. I check for this in every project, and it is usually the most important finding in the write-up.

Leadership

NeuroGIDAS

Founder and co-president, Eastview High School

15+students at meetings
5person team I lead

I started NeuroGIDAS at Eastview to make neuroscience easier for any student to get into. It is our school’s chapter of GIDAS (Genes in Diseases and Symptoms), a miRcore program where students use real genetic data to study how diseases work.

I wrote the proposal that got us a faculty advisor, and I came up with a statewide Brain Cancer Innovation pitch competition that the club is now working to run.

SASA

South Asian Student Association. Founder and co-president, Eastview High School.

20+students in the club
4person team I lead

Growing up, I felt scared to show my culture, and sometimes I did not want to. I started SASA so there would be a place at Eastview where everyone could share theirs openly and feel proud of it.

We bring students together around food, music, festivals and stories, and we keep it open to everyone who wants to learn.
