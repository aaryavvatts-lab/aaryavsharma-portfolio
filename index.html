About

What I actually do

I am a sophomore who got interested in neuroscience through a hand injury a family member had, and it turned into a habit of taking public brain recordings and rebuilding the analysis from the ground up. Seven of those projects are on this site, covering EEG, ECoG, fMRI, MRI, and EMG. I write up what worked, what did not, and why, and I put the code and a working demo online for each one.

At the University of Minnesota I work as a lab assistant in the Moriarty/Webber Lab under Dr. Richa Kaushal, where I help with iPSC culture, macrophage differentiation, and gene editing work. I have also watched two live neurosurgeries under Dr. Anant Naik. Outside of the lab, I am building a smartphone based peripheral nerve health screening concept, and I am active in HOSA, Science Olympiad, and a neuroscience club I helped start at my school.

A pattern across the work

The number before the check, and the number after

The same mistake shows up again and again in this kind of analysis. Split the data the easy way and a model looks great. Split it the way a real device would actually see the world, and most of that greatness disappears. This is something I check for in every project, and it is usually the most important finding in the whole write-up.
